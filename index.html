NEST

Shapeable Charger

A MagSafe phone charger filled with wax, which you press into shape by hand.

Year
2026
Course
Refill: Designing Energy Rituals, ABK Stuttgart
Type
Product, material research
Material
Wax and mineral oil core, two-way stretch knit, PLA ring, Apple-certified MagSafe module
Size
105 × 100 × 68 mm, 340 g
Work
Research, sketching, material tests, prototyping, photography

NEST is a charger for a bedside table or a desk. Its core is wax under a knitted cover, so it takes the shape you press into it and then holds the phone at that angle. A MagSafe module, Apple’s magnetic wireless charger for iPhone, sits in a plastic ring on top.

Project documentation (PDF)Technical document (PDF)

Background and goal

You usually check charging on a screen, but charging also makes heat you can feel.

Phones and chargers show charging with numbers, icons and small status lights, so you have to look to know. Heat is different, because you can notice it with your hand without looking. The project set out to make a charger you understand by touch, and it had four goals.

  • ApproachableIt has no lights and no numbers.
  • TactileThe surface is soft fabric.
  • IntuitiveYou adjust it by pressing, not through settings.
  • PersonalIt keeps the shape you give it.
Process

Wax was chosen for the core after several skins and fillings were tested.

The tests for the outer skin covered latex, TPU (a flexible plastic), silicone and knitted fabric. The tests for the filling covered magnetic beads, steel balls, a thick liquid and wax. Wax gives way when you press it and then stays put under the weight of a phone. The final core is 250 g of microcrystalline wax mixed with 30 g of mineral oil, sealed in a film bag and covered with a two-way stretch knit. A rigid PLA ring on top holds the MagSafe module, and the USB-C port is set into the side of the ring.

Product

You press the phone to the angle you want, and NEST keeps it there.

This works at room temperature, so the phone can lie almost flat or lean up towards you. While it charges, NEST gets slightly warm. That warmth is the only sign that it is working, because the charger has no light and no screen.

Vaporina

Steam Accessory

A steaming insert that lets one pot boil one dish and steam another at the same time.

Year
2025
Client
Alessi (course brief)
Course
Laboratorio di Fondamenti del Progetto, Politecnico di Milano
Material
Stainless steel, silicone
Work
Concept, paper prototypes, renders, final prototype, user book

Vaporina is a stack of stainless steel steaming trays, designed for an Alessi course brief. It sits on a pot of boiling water and is covered by a wavy lid called the “Skirt”.

Background and goal

Alessi’s brief asked for ways to use less energy when cooking.

When water boils on the stove, most of its steam escapes and that heat is lost. Vaporina puts the steam to use: the water at the bottom of the pot cooks one dish, and the trays above it steam a second dish on the same burner. A second goal was that the stack should look good enough to serve from, so the food does not need to move to another dish.

Process

The trays, the lid and the handle were first made in paper.

After the paper prototypes, the design was modelled in 3D and rendered in a range of colours.

Product

One Vaporina fits several pot sizes and lifts out in one piece.

The wavy edge of the “Skirt” sits on pots of the standard diameters. The dancer-shaped handle on top lifts the trays and the lid out together.

Majiroller

Water Roller

A water roller that two children can pull together.

Year
2025
Course
Laboratorio di Design del Prodotto Industriale, Sez. 1, Politecnico di Milano
Focus
Recycled tyres, low cost, durability
Material
50 L rotomoulded HDPE drum, bent steel tube handles, recycled tyres
Work
Concept, 3D, renders

Majiroller is a 50-litre water drum that rolls on two recycled tyres, designed for use in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). It has two long steel handles, shaped for two children to hold.

Background and goal

In the DRC, most water is still carried home by hand.

People carry it in buckets of 15 to 25 kg, often for half an hour or more over mud and sand, and children often do this work. These figures come from the research annex of the course project. The goal was a roller that lets two children share one load and that costs as little as possible to make.

Process

The roller is made from three kinds of parts.

To keep the cost down, it uses a rotomoulded HDPE drum (a seamless plastic tank, the kind used for storing water), two recycled tyres and handles bent from steel tube. The fixings between these parts were worked out in 3D.

Product

Two children pull the 50 kg of water instead of carrying it.

Each child pulls one handle, and the drum rolls between the two tyres over sand and stone. Reusing old tyres is the main idea of the design, because it keeps the cost low.

Field

Generative Visuals

Moving images that a program generates live in the browser, written with WebGL 2.0 and GLSL shaders.

Year
2026
Type
Real-time generative visuals
Tools
WebGL 2.0, GLSL
Method
fBm noise, domain warping, OKLCH colour lookup
Field

The image starts from layered noise that is bent and set in motion.

WebGL lets a web page draw with the graphics card, and a shader is a small program that works out the colour of every pixel. Field is built from four steps.

  • fBmLayers of random noise at different scales are added together (fractal Brownian motion).
  • Domain warpThe noise is used to distort its own coordinates, so the pattern folds into itself.
  • FlowThe pattern drifts in one direction and changes over time.
  • OKLCHThe values become colours through a lookup in OKLCH, a colour space in which equal steps look equally different to the eye.
Image

Texture effects are layered on top of the image.

Halftone screens, grain and colour bands are added at different scales. Depending on which ones are strong, the same motion can look like liquid, like print, or like a screen seen up close.

Interface

I also wrote the control interface.

Every parameter can be changed while the image runs. The program moves gradually from the old value to the new one, so the structure, colour and surface change smoothly instead of switching at once.

Macchina Inutile

Balance Without Purpose

A machine with no use: a light frame of thin wooden sticks, with a small motor that keeps its top turning.

Year
2026
Title
Equilibrio Senza Scopo
Course
Critica del Design, Politecnico di Milano
Type
Kinetic object, short essay
Material
Thin wooden sticks, small electric motor
After
Bruno Munari, Macchine inutili
Object

The machine moves but produces nothing.

It does not cut, carry or make anything, and each turn of the top leaves everything exactly as it was.

Use

The machine can only be watched.

The frame is so fragile that touching it would upset its balance. The viewer cannot use it and has to keep a distance.

Position

The project looks at design without a function.

Design projects usually go from a problem to a result that solves it. This one drops the result and looks only at how a mechanism is put together and kept in balance. It follows Bruno Munari’s Macchine inutili (“useless machines”), hanging objects he began making in the 1930s to be looked at rather than used. The essay argues that an object without a task can make people slow down and look at it closely.
